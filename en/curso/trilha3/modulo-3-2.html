🗣️ Positioning and user journey

If you can’t explain the product in one sentence, no one will explain it for you. And if users don’t feel its value in two minutes, they won’t come back. This module brings the two sides together: what people hear and what they experience.

1

🧷 Positioning in one line

Compare two phrases. The first: "we're an AI-based security platform for modern developers". The second: "antivirus for AI-generated apps". The second is understood in a second because it uses a category everyone knows (antivirus) applied to a new problem (AI-generated apps).

🚕 Why "the Uber of X" stopped working

When Uber was new, the analogy worked: people knew the concept, but it hadn't been used for everything yet. After thousands of "Uber of" something phrases, it became empty. It no longer says anything about your product.

Three simple positioning formulas
FormulaExample
[Familiar category] for [new audience/situation]Antivirus for AI-generated apps
[Result] in [time] without [pain]Contract reviewed in 5 minutes without a lawyer
[Verb] + [object] + [for whom]Turns meetings into tasks for small teams

✓ A phrase that works

  • ✓Fits in one breath.
  • ✓Uses words your customers use.
  • ✓Shows the problem it solves.

✗ A phrase that doesn’t work

  • ✗"Revolutionary," "disruptive," "AI-powered."
  • ✗Technical jargon only you understand.
  • ✗Tries to say everything the product does.

💡 Bar test

Say the phrase to someone outside your field. If they reply, "Oh, I get it," and can repeat it to someone else, the phrase is ready. If they ask, "But what exactly does it do?", go back to the draft.

2

📱 A short video is an elevator pitch

An elevator pitch is the explanation you’d give someone during an elevator ride: 30 seconds or less. If your product works well in a short video, you already have that pitch. A short video is a pitch framework: hook, problem, demo, result.

✓ The same script works for

  • ✓A 20-second social media video.
  • ✓The opening of a sales meeting.
  • ✓Your opening line to an investor.
  • ✓The top of your homepage.

✗ Signs you don’t have a pitch

  • ✗You start with the technology you use.
  • ✗You need a context slide first.
  • ✗The demo takes more than a minute to show anything.

Copy and run

Generate 5 one-line positioning statements and choose the best one using explicit criteria. Paste this into Claude Code or the AI chat you use.

You are an expert in digital product positioning.

My product: <describe what it does in 2-3 sentences>
Primary audience: <who uses it, and in what role/situation>
The pain it solves: <the problem, in the customer’s words>
The result it delivers: <what the person has after using it>

Task:
1. Write 5 one-line positioning statements (no more than 8 words each).
   - Include at least one using the formula "[familiar category] for [new situation]".
   - Not allowed: "revolutionary," "AI-powered," "platform," "the Uber of".
2. Score each statement from 1 to 5 on: clarity for non-experts, memorability, differentiation.
3. Choose the best one and explain why in 2 sentences.
4. Say what question a non-expert might still ask after hearing the chosen statement.
How to check: You get 5 short statements with scores and one winner. Read the winner aloud to someone outside your field; if they can repeat it easily, it’s ready.

💡 Practical tip

Keep the winning phrase somewhere visible. It will show up again in your page headline, social media bio, the start of every video, and email subject lines.

3

👆 Fewer clicks to value

The user journey is the path someone takes from arriving at your product to experiencing its value. The most important metric along that path is time to value: how many clicks and seconds it takes for them to think, "This solves my problem."

New here? Terms in this topic

User journey
The sequence of screens and actions between arrival and the result the person was looking for.
Friction
Anything that takes effort without delivering value: a long signup, email confirmation, welcome screen.
Funnel
A view of the journey as steps, showing how many people move from one step to the next.
Time to value
How long it takes from arrival for the user to see the first useful result.

How to cut clicks

1

Map the current journey

List every screen and click from arrival to the first result. Count them.

2

Mark what doesn’t deliver value

Signup, confirmation, welcome tour, plan selection. Ask whether each step can wait until after the result.

3

Move signup to later

Let people try it first; ask them to create an account when they want to save or download what they generated.

4

Measure again

Compare how many people reach the result before and after the change.

✓ Short journey

  • ✓An input field on the first screen.
  • ✓Result before signup.
  • ✓One main button per screen.

✗ Long journey

  • ✗A five-screen tour before you can start.
  • ✗A credit card to try it.
  • ✗Three equally prominent options on the first screen.

💡 Practical tip

Ask someone to use the product while you watch silently. Each time they hesitate, note the screen. Those hesitations are your friction map.

4

🧪 Validate the interface with a free prototype

A smart way to validate the interface for your main product is to first launch a small, free tool with the same interaction format. If people from different countries, languages, and fields can use it without help, the format is validated.

🔎 Example: the page diagnostic tool

A creator published a free tool: people pasted in the address of their live page and got a full assessment of how it was built. It was one field, one button, and one report.

Weeks later, people were still finding the old post and saying thanks. Users across languages, niches, and technical skill levels used it without asking anything. The conclusion: the format "paste the link, get a diagnosis" works for any audience. That format was reused in the paid product.

✓ A good validation prototype

  • ✓Same input and output flow as the main product.
  • ✓Free and no signup required.
  • ✓Useful on its own, so people share it.

✗ A prototype that teaches you nothing

  • ✗Different format from the product you want to sell.
  • ✗Only friends testing it.
  • ✗No way to see how people used it.

💡 Distribution bonus

A useful free tool is also a hook: people discover you when they use it. Discreetly add a link to the main product at the end of the result.

5

⏱️ Prove value in under two minutes

The bar is simple: Can someone outside your field understand why this is valuable, use it, and see its value in less than two minutes? The ideal action takes just a few steps: click, drop in a file, paste a link, connect an account. That’s it.

✓ Passes the two-minute test

  • ✓A one-sentence explanation on the home screen.
  • ✓A ready-made example to try without your own data.
  • ✓One-click connection to the tools the person already uses.

✗ Fails

  • ✗Asks for an API key before first use.
  • ✗Empty screen, leaving the person to figure out what to do.
  • ✗A result only an expert knows how to interpret.

⚡ How to avoid look-alike demos

Most AI product pages look the same: a big headline, a gradient, and three feature cards. A pattern interrupt means doing something different right away: for example, putting the test field above the fold instead of a "get started" button that leads to sign-up.

Test with a real non-expert

1

Choose someone outside the field

A relative, a neighbor, a friend in another profession.

2

Give them only the address, with no instructions

Start a timer and stay quiet.

3

Stop after two minutes

Ask: "What does this do?" and "Would you use it?" Note where they got stuck.

💡 Practical tip

A preloaded example ("test it with this demo link") solves half the cases. The person sees the value before deciding whether to trust you with their own data.

6

🌀 The content flywheel

A flywheel (a heavy wheel that, once spinning, keeps itself going) is a cycle that feeds itself: the more it spins, the easier it gets to spin. In a content flywheel, usage data becomes articles, articles bring in users, and users generate more data.

New here? Terms in this topic

Anonymize
Remove everything that identifies people or companies, leaving only the aggregated pattern.
SEO
Optimization to appear in search results from engines like Google.
AEO
Optimization to be cited as the answer by answer engines and assistants.
GEO
Optimization to be mentioned by generative AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) when someone asks about the topic.

🔐 Example with the security product

  • •The product finds vulnerabilities in thousands of apps built with AI.
  • •Every week, it automatically groups the most common vulnerabilities without identifying anyone.
  • •It publishes articles like "the 5 vulnerabilities you need to fix in your AI-built app today."
  • •People who are concerned and search for the topic find the article and discover the product without knowing who you are.
🎥

Short-form video

Short-form video platforms don't require followers, only consistency. A new account can get 30,000 views on a video and bring in customers without any call to action.

📝

Automated articles

Periodic reports generated from aggregated data and reviewed before publishing.

🤖

AI answers

Clear content with numbers and lists tends to be cited when someone asks an assistant a question.

⚠️ Privacy is not optional

Anonymization is required and must be covered in the terms of use. Publish aggregated patterns ("37% of the apps analyzed had X"), never identifiable cases. A leak in your own content destroys the product, especially if the product is about security.
